# Security and hardening audit — 2026-11-05

**Scope:** the P(-1) pass for 0.2.2, over the whole tree at 0.2.1 (Cyrius 6.7.18, agnosai 2.2.3):
17,516 lines of `src/`, the WebGUI, and what `src/` reaches in `lib/`.

**Method:** the eight-point process in `first-party-standards.md`, by three independent reviewers on
a frozen snapshot (security; correctness and robustness; performance and memory). Each finding was
then checked against the code, and most were reproduced on a loopback server. The repairs had a
second review of their own, and a live replay of the reproductions. No real browser was used, and the
OTLP export path and agnosai's internals were not audited.

**Severity:** CRITICAL remote or privilege escalation; HIGH moderate effort; MEDIUM specific
conditions; LOW defence in depth.

## Result

**No authentication or authorisation bypass, and no memory corruption.** The serious findings are
unauthenticated denial of service and lost crew results. All of them are repaired in 0.2.2 except the
items under *Open*, each of which is on the roadmap.

Performance repairs, none a defect: the engine's unread audit chain is off (9.6 KB per crew, and
re-hashing past 10,000 entries); an authenticated request runs one statement, not two; `GET /audit`
reads a counter rather than scanning under the audit lock; a listing row reads two integers without
building a JSON object; eviction no longer allocates.

Also repaired while reading: a failed bind now logs `server failed` with its step and errno. It used
to print `listening` and exit 1.

## The repairs' own review

A second, independent review of the diff, which ran the real binary under load, found five defects in
the first version of these repairs. All are fixed in 0.2.2, each with a regression test that fails
without the fix, and each was re-measured with the reviewer's harness.

| Severity | Defect | Fix | Measured after |
|---|---|---|---|
| HIGH | C1's first fix held only to about 75 crews/s. Retirement ran after the 11.5 ms write, so the collector managed about 50 crews a second, and at 200/s the eviction fired anyway. Evicting `unknown` then made lost crews answer 404: of 6,000 crews, 1,005 completed. The suite's test slept 1 ms per crew and could not see it. | Collecting and writing are separate, and the writes are time-boxed. `unknown` is not evicted. | 6,000 at 219/s: all completed. |
| HIGH | A cancelled crew could lose its results if one thread saw the close between another's last look and its retirement: 45 of 4,000 under four spinning refreshers. | The thread that retires looks once more first. | 0 of 4,000 |
| MEDIUM | A successful cancel could answer 409 and skip its audit record, when a refresh latched its CANCELLED first. | That latch is this cancel succeeding. | 0 of 4,000 |
| MEDIUM | ADR 0021's "bounded by its largest request" was false: bodies that each grew by 16 B kept 880 KB per request, taking one worker from 75 to 340 MB. | A worker's arena and chunks are at least the body cap. | 300 rising bodies: RSS flat at 72 MB |
| LOW | Splitting the crew write dropped the in-flight row when the listing row failed, leaving the repair nothing to work from. | The in-flight row stays. | Regression test |

Deferring the writes created one obligation, also met: a graceful stop writes the backlog. 1,500 crews
with SIGTERM right after the last was accepted took 10.6 s, and all read `completed` after a restart.

## Worst-case memory, for the record

ADR 0021 makes a worker hold its largest request. At the defaults that is about 20 MiB per worker if
each meets a 1 MiB parse tree, so 320 MB in the worst case. A body cannot take it further; a response
over 1 MiB can, slowly, and that behaviour of `_arena_grow` is filed with cyrius. A live replay of the
reproductions on the repaired binary held flat through every read phase.

## Checked and clean

- **Input validation:** a 2,500-case mutation fuzz of crew, definition and plugin bodies and 13,220
  mutated crew bodies gave no 500 and no crash. Malformed HTTP is handled, and deep JSON is bounded.
- **Buffers:** every `var x[N]` in `src/` against its largest access; the audit slot layout and the
  ring and table bounds.
- **Syscalls and pointers:** request-path returns are handled; no untrusted raw dereference.
- **Arena lifetime:** no request-arena data outlives its request.
- **Locks and the store:** every lock pairs on every return, there is no nested store and ledger
  lock, and statements and result sets are freed on every early return.
- **Command injection and path traversal:** no exec in `src/`; the only file opens use operator
  configuration; `/ui/plugins/{id}` is a table lookup.
- **Known-vulnerability patterns:** no SQL concatenation, no header injection, no request smuggling
  (duplicate Content-Length or Host refused, Transfer-Encoding answered 501, no keep-alive), no CORS
  grant.
- **JWT:** `alg:none`, an empty key, tampering and a flipped bit all answer 401; the signature is
  compared in constant time, on encoded text, before any parse.
- **API keys:** a bucket hit is re-verified in constant time over all rows.
- **Authz:** all 26 route ids are classified; path tricks answer 404 and unknown methods 405.
- **Tenant isolation:** crews and plugin documents are scoped, and 404s match across tenants.
- **Login:** `X-Forwarded-For`, `X-Real-IP` and `Forwarded` do not bypass the limit.
- **Secrets:** no password or token in a log.
- **Host guard and CSRF:** 18 Host forms; Content-Type tricks answer 415.
- **Plugin gate:** ungranted routes and other plugins' data are refused, and the CSP is hash-only.
- **WebGUI:** all 36 `innerHTML` sinks in Swarm Command are escaped or fed normalised data.
